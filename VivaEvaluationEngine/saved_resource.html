Verdict

Every critical scoring defect from the last audit is fixed

An architecture audit dated 15 August 2026 sits in docs/VIVA_ENGINE_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md. Its verdict was blunt: "What you do not yet have is a trustworthy viva grader." It listed five critical defects that systematically mis-ranked candidates, and told you not to treat engine scores as final until they were fixed.

I checked each one against the code as it stands today. All five are fixed, along with most of the high and medium findings. This is the single most important thing to know about the current state of the system, and it is the strongest evidence in the project that the engineering process works.

Execution order

What one uploaded video goes through

Five stages, strictly sequential in viva_service.py::analyze_video_file. Stages 3 and 4 are network calls to Groq that degrade to deterministic fallbacks when no key is configured.

01

Video track

services/analysis_service.py
FaceLandmarker @ 10 fps→ adaptive-EAR blinks→ frames @ 1 fps→ gaze / head / mouth→ denoise · CLAHE · gamma · unsharp→ EfficientNet-B0 ×2→ 3-frame vote

MediaPipe Tasks FaceLandmarker supplies 478 points (468 mesh + iris) at 10 fps for blink counting; scoring frames sample at 1 fps and reuse the nearest landmark set within 0.6 s. Blink detection uses max(0.15, 0.55 × session median EAR) rather than the guide's fixed 0.25, with a documented reason: MediaPipe's 6-point EAR sits lower than the classic pixel EAR. Crops are enhanced before classification and only empty or sub-48 px crops are rejected — on a single-student camera there is no second face to fall back to.

02

Audio track

services/viva_analysis.py
ffmpeg → 16 kHz mono→ diarize + pick student→ Whisper on mixed→ filter to student→ collapse examiner gaps→ Praat + librosa→ SER subprocess→ transcript features

The ordering is the clever part. Whisper runs on the mixed track so word timestamps stay aligned to real time; words are only afterwards filtered to the student's diarized speaker. collapse_excluded_gaps then subtracts examiner intervals from the word timeline, so a long panel question cannot be scored as the student hesitating. Acoustics and speech emotion run on a separately written student-only WAV. Whisper weights are explicitly released before SER loads, to leave RAM headroom.

03

LLM judge

services/llm_judge.pyAdvisory only
assemble feature JSON→ Groq chat, temp 0.2→ schema validate→ retry once→ formula fallback

Never sees raw audio or video — only numbers already computed upstream, which keeps the call small and the score reproducible. Returns three criteria 0–10 with citation-style justifications. Heuristic speech emotion is stripped from the payload and replaced with a provenance stub so the model cannot lean on it. Every response ships its deterministic fallback alongside for comparison.

04

Conversation & Q&A relevance

conversation_understanding.py · qa_relevance.pyAdvisory only
turns + IDs→ windowed structure pass→ merge overlaps→ pair Q↔A→ relevance per pair, 4 threads

Two chained LLM roles. The first classifies the transcript into presentation, panel question, student answer, follow-up, interruption and instruction — using only supplied turn IDs, never inventing timestamps, with 8-minute overlapping windows and recursive splitting to stay inside a 40k-character budget. The second judges each question–answer pair for whether the answer addresses the question. Its prompt explicitly forbids grading, judging intelligence, or assessing technical correctness. Heuristic pairing remains as fallback when Groq is unavailable.

05

Assessment & persistence

assessment_scoring.py → viva_marks.pyAuthoritative
canonical features→ validity gates→ 3 families → /100→ grade band→ Mongo insert→ auto-publish

Everything upstream reduces to one canonical feature vector, gated for validity, then scored. A flat training_features row is emitted alongside — the schema for a future trained model that does not yet exist. The result is written to vivamark.marks and immediately auto-published in presentation-only mode with human_published: false.

Central architecture

Three scoring systems run; one of them counts

Every analysis produces three independent sets of numbers that disagree by design. Only the first is the mark. This is the easiest thing about the system to misread, and it is worth stating plainly in the dissertation before anyone asks.

What Stage-1 refuses to use

Exclusion list

Each exclusion is a defensible research position, written into the module docstring rather than left implicit. Together they are the most publishable thing in the codebase:

  • Facial and speech emotion ratios — carried on the feature vector as evidence, kept out of the mark on the stated principle that emotion ≠ achievement. This also sidesteps the PRD's own documented risk about cultural expression bias, and the SER module's own warning that projected presentation speech is frequently labelled "angry" by IEMOCAP-trained models.
  • Transcript length — word_count/80 and segment count are computed but excluded, because talking more is not talking better.
  • Absolute pitch — the legacy pitch_score penalised distance from 180 Hz, which scores voices rather than delivery. Only pitch stability survives.
  • RMS loudness — excluded as a recording-level artefact; only energy consistency (inverse coefficient of variation) is kept, and it is stored rather than scored.
  • Heuristic speech emotion — capped at 0.4 confidence and flagged out of every scoring path, so a silent model failure cannot masquerade as a genuine neutral reading.
  • LLM scores and UI sliders — neither the judge nor the examiner's rubric widgets can move the AI performance number.

Three different numbers all called "engagement"

Documented, still confusing

Rather than collapsing them, engagement_metrics publishes all three with explicit provenance. A reasonable answer to a genuinely awkward situation — but three plausible numbers still ship in every response:

Metric IDScaleSourceUsed by
stage1_cnn_engagement0–1Per-frame CNN blended meanOfficial mark
diagnostic_engagement0–100scoring.compute_engagement_scoreUI display
feature_complete_engagement0–1engagement_scoring blendDiagnostic

Worth knowing about the official one: the CNN's per-frame score is level × confidence + 0.5 × (1 − confidence), so an uncertain model is pulled toward 0.5. The official engagement family is therefore confidence-shrunk toward the midpoint and will rarely reach either extreme. Defensible, but it compresses the score range in a way that matters when you calibrate against human marks.

All four documents

Where the build stands against its own design

The four documents were written in sequence and do not fully agree with each other. The March "Multimodal AI" concept paper is the broadest; the PRD narrows it; the Architecture Document specifies it; the FER Guide drills into the face module. Reading all four together shows the build diverging from the earliest documents in ways that are mostly deliberate.

Facial Expression Recognition Guide

Formulas demoted

This is the most directly implemented document — and the most quietly superseded:

  • Its headline engagement formula is now diagnostic only. The guide's §07 weighted sum — 0.30 emotion + 0.30 gaze + 0.20 head + 0.10 blink + 0.10 LSTM — is implemented exactly, in both compute_engagement_score and ENGAGEMENT_FEATURE_WEIGHTS. But Stage-1 ignores it and uses the CNN mean alone. The guide's compute_confidence_from_face is likewise implemented and likewise excluded. The face module the guide specifies no longer determines the mark.
  • DeepFace was never used. The guide's §04 lists HSEmotion as the higher-accuracy alternative, so the substitution is sanctioned — but it recommends enet_b2_8 (EfficientNet-B2), and the code loads EfficientNet-B0. The checkpoint is named hsemotion_improved.pt, no training script or evaluation report exists in the repo, and the original enet_b0_8_best_afew.pt sits unused beside it. The ">85% on FER2013" claim cannot currently be substantiated for the model that actually runs.
  • EAR threshold deviates with justification. The guide specifies 0.25; the code uses an adaptive max(0.15, 0.55 × median) and documents why in a config comment.
  • Head pose was specified as solvePnP Euler angles. The code uses landmark-distance proxies and says so.

Multimodal AI for Viva Examination Evaluation (March)

Largest divergence
  • Hand and gesture analysis is absent entirely. This document treats MediaPipe Hands — 21 keypoints, self-touch detection, fidgeting index, arm-crossing — as a first-class third modality worth 10% of its illustrative contribution pie. Nothing was built. The PRD later moved it to Phase 4, so this is a scope decision rather than a miss, but the earliest document's premise was audio + video + gesture.
  • Its preprocessing instruction "trim silence and interviewer voice" is exactly what diarization now does — arguably the clearest case of an early design note being properly realised.
  • Its explainability example — "Candidate spoke slowly and avoided eye contact, indicating low confidence" — is precisely the output format llm_judge.py produces.
  • Its entire evaluation chapter is unexecuted: subject-independent splits, K-fold by person, UAR/F1/AUC, calibration, ablation per modality, demographic bias tests. All require a labelled dataset that does not exist.

Built beyond every document

Genuine contribution

Four capabilities in the code have no counterpart in any of the four documents. For a research submission these are the most defensible parts of the contribution, because they answer problems the literature-derived design did not anticipate:

  • Mouth-correlated speaker assignment. Every document assumes a clean single-speaker recording. Real vivas have a panel. Identifying the student by lip movement rather than loudness or speaking time is a specific, testable idea — and the code goes further, refusing loudness by name in a comment.
  • Over-clustering collapse. looks_like_same_voice_overcluster detects when k-means has chopped one talker into fake alternating speakers — rapid A/B/A/B turns, or two clusters with indistinguishable mouth-movement profiles — and merges them back. This is the kind of failure mode you only find by running real recordings.
  • Lip-motion validity checking. A cheap integrity check that the transcript plausibly came from the visible person.
  • Relevance-without-correctness Q&A analysis. The middle ground between no question-awareness and a full reference-answer bank — the direct, defensible answer to "why did you drop technical accuracy?"

Fixed

Whisper now defaults to medium

transcribe.py:24 · viva_analysis.py:25 · .env.example:19

A third of the official mark comes from the transcript family, so it inherited whatever Whisper's error rate was. resolve_whisper_model_size() now normalises VIVA_WHISPER_MODEL with medium as the default, and the resolved size is echoed back in the response as audio_analysis.whisper_model — so a report can state which model produced its transcript rather than leaving it implicit. Fixed

Fusion weights unified, and the fused mark now actually reaches the screen

officialMark.ts · ScoreOverview.tsx · VivaPage.tsx · assessment_scoring.py

The consolidation was right, and better than what this audit suggested. officialMark.ts became a single client rule reading fusion weights from the server rather than hardcoding 50/50, and ReportPrintView stopped recomputing independently. That closed the original finding.

Verifying it, though, surfaced a chain of three bugs that made the fused mark invisible — the stored value was always correct, but nothing on screen or in the printed report ever showed it. All three are now fixed:

  • The autoSaved short-circuit. Every valid analysis auto-publishes, so auto_published was always true and the guard returned the without-technical score, making the live preview unreachable. An examiner moving the slider saw the number sit still. Removed from the disjunct, with a comment explaining why it must not come back.
  • handlePublish discarded the response. publishVivaMark() returns the recomputed score and grade; the result was dropped and only setPublished(true) ran, so the stale value survived a successful publish. Now merged into both analysisResult and assessment, and the success toast names the saved mark.
  • ScoreOverview was never migrated despite the handoff listing it as done — it still resolved the mark itself and was not passed the mode, technical accuracy, or published flag. Now on the shared rule, and it renders "Preview with technical accuracy — publish to save" while a draft is unsaved.

A fourth problem only appeared once the preview became reachable. Because every analysis auto-publishes in WITHOUT_TECHNICAL_ACCURACY, its fusion block reports the weights it actually applied — weight_ai 1.0, weight_technical 0.0. Previewing with those multiplies technical accuracy by zero, so the slider would still have appeared dead:

analyze response, before
"fusion": { "weight_ai": 1.0, "weight_technical": 0.0 }
preview = 1.0 x 67.0  +  0.0 x 90.0  =  67.0   ← slider does nothing

analyze response, after
"fusion": { "weight_ai": 1.0, "weight_technical": 0.0,
            "with_technical": { "weight_ai": 0.5,
                                "weight_technical": 0.5 } }
preview = 0.5 x 67.0  +  0.5 x 90.0  =  78.5   ← matches the server exactly

The server now publishes fusion.with_technical — the weights a technical publish would apply — so the client previews from scorer constants it never has to hold itself. Verified end to end: a draft preview and a real MODE_WITH publish both produce 78.5 from the same inputs. Two regression tests in test_assessment_scoring.py pin the contract, so a future weight change cannot silently desynchronise the preview from the stored mark.

Auto-publish no longer contradicts the integrity rule — it's now structurally impossible for the vivas that need review

main.py::viva_analyze · VivaPage.tsx · EvaluationPanel.tsx · ReportPrintView.tsx

The prior fix was going to be "make the provisional state visible" — cosmetic. The actual fix changes when the assessment type is decided: the examiner now picks Non-technical or Technical from a tab pair before uploading or recording, not after seeing a score. That single ordering change closes the contradiction by construction rather than by disclosure:

  • Server — /api/viva-analyze takes the choice as a form field. Only WITHOUT_TECHNICAL_ACCURACY auto-publishes; WITH_TECHNICAL_ACCURACY never does — the mark sits as an unpublished draft until an examiner enters a technical score and hits publish. Pinned with a new test, test_technical_mode_never_auto_publishes.
  • Client — the old post-hoc radio switcher in EvaluationPanel (which let the mode be flipped after the AI score was already visible — a softer version of the same problem) is gone, replaced by a locked, read-only badge.
  • The exact sentence this audit flagged — the printed report's footer claiming every score was "reviewed by the evaluator prior to publishing" — is no longer a blanket claim. It now states the true, mode-specific fact per report: technical reports say the score was human-reviewed and fused; non-technical reports say plainly that it was automatic and disclosed as such.

Not yet committed — this fix (5 files: main.py, test_viva_http.py, VivaPage.tsx, EvaluationPanel.tsx, ReportPrintView.tsx) is verified (all 210 tests pass, client builds clean) but sits in the working tree, not yet staged.

Low

Smaller defects worth a cleanup pass

  • Missing confidence becomes maximum confidence. _normalize_confidence(None) returns 1.0 in both detectors, so an unparseable model output is recorded as fully certain rather than unknown.
  • Technical accuracy cannot be set to 0. The slider renders technicalAccuracy ?? 0, so it sits visually at zero while the state is null and publish stays blocked; dragging to 0 may not fire a change. An examiner who genuinely wants 0/10 has to move the slider away and back.
  • Blob URL leak on one path. The pre-analysis "Remove" button clears videoPreview without URL.revokeObjectURL; the post-analysis one revokes correctly.
  • API key accepted as a query parameter on REST. Necessary for the WebSocket, unnecessary for require_api_key, and query strings land in access logs.
  • tempo_bpm is beat-tracking run on speech — a music feature with no meaning here. Harmless (unused in scoring) but it appears in the UI's advanced metrics.
  • Stale references. requirements.txt names a different default SER model than the code uses; face_landmarker.py annotates np.ndarray without importing numpy (safe only because of from __future__ import annotations); enet_b0_8_best_afew.pt is 16 MB of unused checkpoint, and all three .pt files are committed to git rather than LFS.
  • The printed report includes the full student and panel transcript with no consent notice, and that document is what would be filed.
Strength

The code tells the truth about its own weaknesses

Worth recording explicitly, because it is unusual and it is defensible in a viva of your own. The engine self-reports uncertainty rather than hiding it: gaze and head pose both carry "validated": false with named reasons (no_looking_away_recording, no_turn_tilt_recording) because no ground-truth recording exists to validate them against. The SER module attaches a domain note warning that IEMOCAP-trained models frequently label projected presentation speech as "angry" and that this is not a viva affect diagnosis. sentence_completion_is_heuristic ships with every transcript. The temporal model refuses to emit a number without a trained checkpoint. Speech emotion runs in a subprocess specifically so a native crash cannot take down the server. None of these were required; each one makes the system harder to misread.

Research position

What the project still needs

The engineering is now well ahead of the evidence. Every capability claim across all four documents — Kappa ≥ 0.75, ≥85% agreement, ≤10% WER, ≥85% FER accuracy — is a target with no measurement behind it. For a research submission that gap now matters more than any remaining feature.

Collect the dual-marked set

Blocking everything

Nothing else can be validated without it, and it is the only thing standing between the current system and the trained fusion model all four documents specify. flatten_canonical_features already emits exactly the row a Random Forest would consume — the schema is waiting, the labels are not. Even 30–50 human-scored sessions would convert several unverified claims into measured ones and unblock the Kappa analysis.

Evaluate the emotion model, or stop claiming a number

Credibility

FER2013 has a standard held-out split and is a Kaggle download away. A single evaluation run on hsemotion_improved.pt either substantiates the ">85%" claim inherited from the guide or replaces it with the real figure. Right now the checkpoint has no provenance in the repo at all.

Test at target duration

Pilot risk

The PRD targets 20-minute sessions and a 5-minute end-to-end budget. Live capture now caps at 10 minutes, so a full-length viva must go through the upload tab — and nothing in the repo shows that path exercised at 20 minutes against the 1 GB cap and 600 s timeout. One honest end-to-end run before students are in front of it.
